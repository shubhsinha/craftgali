1aThe brief: what I changed and why
Design rationale · v1

Two theme systems, one quiet shell

You picked both, separate — so the app and the storefront are governed by two different theme engines that never touch. That single decision drives everything below.

Shell
I removed the glassmorphism. Frosted panels sitting over artwork desaturate it and add a grey cast to every thumbnail — the opposite of what an art marketplace needs. The shell is now lime-washed paper: flat warm ground, hairline rules, one soft shadow tier. Your four hues survive as the buyer's accent, but they only touch interactive elements, never surfaces.
Motif
Rethought, as you asked. The all-over tapestry (28 state landmarks, Warli, Gond, jali) was doing craft-as-wallpaper — dense, uniform, and legible at no zoom level. It is replaced by one mark per theme, drawn huge and cropped, plus a recurring hairline thread rule that segments every page. Heritage becomes structure instead of texture. The landmark wheel isn't deleted — it moves to the landing hero as a single deliberate object, where it earns its detail.
Storefronts
Four full skins — grid, type pairing, motif and texture all change, not just hue. Each is named for a craft material and tuned to a kind of maker. See 1d.

From the PRD
Three things I designed around rather than decorating: the slot meter ("7 of 10 pieces live" — worded as concurrent, per §4.2), the fee preview shown while writing, not at payment (§4.5), and the in-chat advance-payment interstitial (§7.3). Full storefront theming is a Pro Studio ₹599 feature per §4.2, so the editor in 1g gates two of the four skins.
Assumed
You left content blank, so artists, titles and prices are carried over from your HTML where they existed (Meera's Clay Atelier, Rohan's Fine Art Studio, Monsoon Serenade in Oils…) and invented plausibly elsewhere. Artwork is a photo placeholder — I can't generate images. Give me real pieces and I'll drop them in. Slogans are yours, verbatim.

The making of

I started this on the 4th of July, the first real day of the monsoon in Pune, sitting under the awning of a chai stall on Baner Road because I couldn't get home. The colour of wet tarmac at that hour is not grey. It's a warm violet with orange sodium light sitting on top of it, and it lasts about twenty minutes.

Nine sittings, two of them scraped back entirely. The final glaze layer is Prussian blue over burnt sienna, which is what gives the puddles their depth. Unframed — I'd rather you choose the frame than pay me to guess.
